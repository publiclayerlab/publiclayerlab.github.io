Research on digital and administrative arrangements that shape public and civic life.

Public Layer Lab is a public-interest research lab that investigates how rules, organisations, data, software, infrastructure, suppliers, and administrative processes come together in consequential public and civic arrangements.

The concern is not technology in isolation. These arrangements can shape how authority and responsibility are organised, what institutions are able to understand and control, what becomes visible or recognised, and how people and organisations can participate, question decisions, seek correction, or contribute to changing the systems that affect them.

The work includes public institutions, civic infrastructures, and socially relevant organisations. Automated and AI-assisted processes are part of this wider field where they alter how information is interpreted, decisions are prepared, responsibility is distributed, or outcomes can be understood and challenged.

From interfaces to institutional arrangements

Looking beyond the interface.

Public Layer Lab examines arrangements at this wider level: reconstructing how they work across institutional, organisational, and technical boundaries and making consequential choices, dependencies, and uncertainties easier to see.

A simple example

A digital form can be fast and still be difficult to question.

A benefits or support form may make an application quicker while also shaping which situations fit the available categories, what evidence is accepted, when a case moves to review, and whether an applicant can understand or challenge the outcome.

Looking beyond the interface raises wider questions. Who defines the categories? Where does responsibility sit? What systems and organisations does the process depend on? What information is recorded or made visible? Who has the practical capacity to correct the process when something goes wrong?

Current focus: source-grounded investigation, institutional reconstruction, and public explanation of digital and administrative arrangements of public or social importance.

Particular attention goes to arrangements where questions of authority, institutional reliance, civic agency, public understanding, accountability, or practical possibilities for correction and change are difficult to see from an interface, workflow, procurement arrangement, or formal procedure alone.

Specific analyses and other materials are published when they are sufficiently sourced and ready for responsible public description.
